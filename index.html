Section Three · The Dossier

Six behaviors Big Roo would rather you filed under “cute.”

Every item below is a real, documented kangaroo behavior. The alarm is ours. The behavior is theirs.

🚘

They own the roads

Kangaroos account for the overwhelming majority of animal-related vehicle collision claims in Australia — consistently reported at around four in five. No other animal is close. This is not an accident rate. This is a strategy.

Australian insurer collision claim reporting
🏊

They practice drowning

A cornered kangaroo will wade into deep water and hold a pursuing dog under with its forelimbs. Naturalists call this an anti-predator behavior. We call it a technique, because it is one.

Documented anti-predator behavior
🥊

They box. On purpose.

Male kangaroos grapple, lock forearms, and deliver full-body kicks braced on the tail. They are the only large animal that trains for this recreationally, against each other, for years, for fun.

Standard male-male combat behavior
☠

They have killed people

Fatal kangaroo attacks are rare but real — including a widely reported 2022 death in Western Australia involving a kangaroo the victim had been keeping. “Rare” is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

Reported fatal incidents, Australia
🛡

They pause the reproduction

Through embryonic diapause, a female can hold a fertilized embryo in suspended development until conditions improve. They can literally schedule the next generation. Nothing that can do that is not planning something.

Marsupial reproductive biology
🏕

They move for free

Hopping stores and returns energy elastically through the tendons, so faster hopping costs a kangaroo almost no extra energy. They can outlast you at speeds you cannot sustain for ninety seconds.

Locomotion energetics research
